## Video format standard (Step, Nov 4)

Every clip follows this format. The sample is `~/Movies/STEPBAE-WMDA-2016/clips/02-stick-to-your-day-job-v3.mp4`.

- **Website on screen the whole time:** stepbae.com inside the video area, plus on every end card.
- **A shout-out to another professional in every clip.** They get a name label when they appear, and a shout-out card at the end with their photo, credentials, practice and current teaching event or website. Tag them in the post.
- **Who is the star (Step, Nov 4):** contestants are the stars of commentary clips. When Eugene is the funny one, he is the star. **Step gets slightly less than half the spotlight** across the series (count by screen time and by which clips are "about" whom). Use split screens and zooms on whoever is mentioned or reacting.
- **Roasts must read as obvious jokes:** stickers, "JUST KIDDING!", a "WE LOVE YOU ___" moment with hearts. No haters.
- **Smooth talk:** cut stutters, restarts and long pauses. Re-transcribe the edit to check no words are clipped.
- **Layout:** top band (event label + hook), 3:4 video, word-by-word captions in the brand fonts, dark bottom strip kept clear for the app's buttons. End: shout-out card (2.5 s), then the STEPBAE card (logo, proof point, stepbae.com). Commentary keeps playing under both cards.

## The approved template (clip #02, Nov 4)

Reference file: `~/Movies/STEPBAE-WMDA-2016/clips/02-stick-to-your-day-job-v3.mp4`. Build scripts: `~/Movies/STEPBAE-WMDA-2016/v2/edit02.py` (cut and layout) and `compose02.py` (graphics, captions, stickers, cards).

- **Frame (1080x1920):** dark ink background. Top band: gold label (event · "LIVE COMMENTARY") plus a white hook line. Video 1080x1440 (3:4) below it. A dark strip at the bottom, kept clear for the app's buttons.
- **stepbae.com** in white with a soft shadow, inside the bottom of the video area, the whole time. No box.
- **Captions:** Montserrat ExtraBold, word by word, the current word in gold. Check names and terms (AACD, DTG, e.max, Brumm).
- **Cuts:** remove stutters, false starts and long pauses. Check by transcribing short windows, because Whisper drops the first words of long audio.
- **Spotlight:** the featured professional or Eugene is the star; Step gets slightly less than half. Split screens (Step on top, the other person below) when two people talk. Zoom in on whoever is named, and track faces when the camera pans.
- **Hold on the featured person:** at least 2–3.5 s on screen when they are introduced or named (slow motion is fine), with a name label: name, then practice · tagline.
- **Roasts read as obvious jokes:** a "JUST KIDDING!" tag, 😂🤣 stickers, then a slow-motion "WE LOVE YOU [NAME]" moment with hearts. Funny asides get a cute tag and stickers placed next to the person, about 2 s.
- **No sign blurring:** leave background signs as they are (blur looked bad).
- **End:** a 2.5 s shout-out card (photo in a gold ring, name, credentials, practice and tagline, next course or website), then a 2.4 s STEPBAE card (logo with STEPBAE the same width as TOOTHMASTER, a proof point, stepbae.com).
- **Length (Step, Nov 4, updated):** no hard cap. Most clips run 30–45 s and some run near 60 s for variety. If the material is strong enough to hold 2 minutes, make it 2 minutes and split it into parts (Part 1, Part 2…) for the platforms that need shorter videos. Quality decides the length, not the clock.
- **Over 60 s → split into parts (Step, Nov 4).** A strong story that runs past 60 s (e.g. 85 s) becomes Part 1, Part 2, … each 60 s or less. **Split on a cliffhanger**: end a part on a question or setup ("he's pulling something out of his 007 box…", "some dry, some wet, what's that all about?") and open the next part with the reveal. No "next part" teaser text on the shout-out card (Step, Nov 4: "not good"). What plays under the cards in a cliffhanger part is still open; revisit with Step.
- **Recap at the start of Part 2, 3, … (Step, Nov 4).** Each later part opens with a 4–7 s recap of the previous part's last beat (usually the cliffhanger line, with live footage), labeled "PREVIOUSLY · PART 1", then goes straight into the reveal. Example: Porcelain Secrets Part 2 opens with "That's why all this grinding is happening. Oh, wait a minute, he's pulling off of his 007 box." (6.4 s).
- **Series naming (decided Nov 4).** All parts share one series name in the small gold label (e.g. "WMDA 2016 · PORCELAIN SECRETS · PART 1"), so viewers recognize the series and look for the next part. Each part keeps its own big hook line, because the hook is what stops the scroll ("Why we grind porcelain" / "It's never just a shade tab").
- **Show who is talking (Step, Nov 4).** Don't stay on one contestant too long. When Step or Eugene speaks, put the speaker on screen: split screen with the speaker on top and the featured professional's work below. **Lips must match the words (Step, Nov 4).** A person appears on screen only in live footage from the same moment as the audio. No still photos of a speaker ("Eugene is talking but not moving, looks bad"). No footage of someone talking at another time, including a contestant's intro video under Step's voice. If the speaker isn't on camera, show what the camera saw (the work) as a voice-over, with a "EUGENE / STEP speaking" tag: EUGENE top-left, STEP top-right (matching where they stand on camera), name about 60 px, "speaking" about 40 px (Step, Nov 5). Every piece is cut to whole frames so audio and video can't drift (`c06/edit06.py`).
- **Split screens: keep the contestant panel wide (Step, Nov 4).** In a top/bottom split, the contestant's panel takes at least a 1440×960 area of the source frame (whole bench and person in view), never a tight 1080×720 or smaller crop. A deliberate joke zoom (Mike laughing in #02) is the exception.
- **Smooth and fluent (Step, Nov 4):** cut every stutter, repeated word, false start and dead pause ("Ryan, Ryan Lim", "is a, is a", "so that's- that's where"). Emphasis repeats that sound intentional stay ("very, very young", "I couldn't match it. I could not match it."). How to check: join the kept audio, then transcribe it twice, once plain and once with a filler prompt. A repeat that shows up in both is real; one that shows up only with the prompt is usually invented, so confirm it with the loudness profile before cutting (`c16/splice.py`, `c16/rms.sh`).
- **Captions must sync with the voice (Step, Nov 4).** The transcriber's own word times drift by up to 1.5 s, so they are not used for timing. Text comes from the transcription (chunked + whole-clip passes, aligned). Timing comes from the audio: speech phrases are found from loudness, words are assigned to phrases in order (matched to each phrase's length), then spread over the moments with sound (`v2/captions.py`: `speech_phrases` + `fit_to_speech`). Read the full caption list before delivering.
- **End sequence (Step, Nov 4):** shout-out card (2.5 s) → STEPBAE card (~2.4 s) → optional promo card (~2.4–2.8 s). The promo card is a ready-made full-frame image laid over the last commentary. Current promo: `~/Movies/STEPBAE-WMDA-2016/promos/aes2027/card.png`. It is Step's AES 2027 talk, "Problem Solvers: Make Big Problems Small," Thu Feb 25, 2027, 1:30–1:45 PM, AES 72nd Annual Scientific Meeting, Chicago Marriott Downtown Magnificent Mile (aes-tmj.org; register at aes-tmj.glueup.com/event/169267/register/). The card has the AES banner, Step's black-and-white headshot (`step_headshot_bw.jpg`, Step's pick; the name is written Step Toothmaster Bae, CDT, DTG, with no quotes because it isn't a nickname) and a "CATCH STEP LIVE" label. Rebuild it from `card.ass` in that folder. Take it off clips after Feb 25, 2027.
- **No silence, ever (Step, Nov 4).** The audio never stops. The commentary keeps running under the featured photo, the shout-out card and the STEPBAE card. Captions stop the moment the shout-out card appears, so the cards stay clean (the audio keeps going). Pick the next lines of commentary that praise the featured person and end the clip on a closing line (the #17 ending is "And now he's back here as a champion." on the STEPBAE card). Fade out only in the last 0.6 s. Card lengths follow the words: about 2.5 s for the shout-out and 2.5–4 s for the STEPBAE card. In the build, the end lines are dark-plate pieces in `edit*.py` and the cards are laid on top in `compose*.py` (see `c17/`).

- **No long takes (Step, Nov 5):** while the clip plays, the picture changes at least once every 5 seconds: a camera cut, a new photo, or a reframe. A still photo never holds longer than 5 s.
- **...but it must look edited by an editor (Step, Nov 5, after the first try looked choppy).** The first version flipped wide/tight every 4 s on the clock, often mid-word, cropped the centre of the frame (sometimes an empty door), and gave every 4 s piece its own audio fade. Never again. The rules (`rhythm.py` for `mk.py` clips, `punch.py` for the older builds):
  - **Where:** a reframe goes on a jump cut left by pause trimming, since the reframe hides the jump. Otherwise it goes on the quietest moment between words, 2.5–5 s into the hold. Never mid-word, and never closer than 1.5 s to another change. Real camera cuts are found automatically and reset the clock.
  - **What:** the second framing is a real close-up on the faces in the shot (macOS Vision face detection, `tools/faces`), not a centre crop. A letterboxed wide shot cuts to a 3:4 shot of the people in it. In a split screen, each panel tightens on its own person. With no faces (hands, work), use a gentle push-in.
  - **Audio:** one continuous track for the whole body (`body_audio.wav`). Nothing happens to the sound where only the picture changes; a 10 ms fade only where words were actually cut.
  - **Check:** look at a 1-frame-per-second sheet before delivering: every close-up should show a face, and no shot should run past 5 s.
